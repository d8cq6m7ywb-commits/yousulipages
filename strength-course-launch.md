## 1. Positioning

### The problem the course solves

Endurance athletes get strength wrong in one of two ways:

1. **They skip it.** "More miles is more specific." Then the calf goes at
   week 9 of the marathon build, or the shoulder at month 3 of swim volume.
2. **They do cardio in the gym.** High-rep, light-load circuits that add
   fatigue, blunt key sessions, and build nothing the sport can use.

The evidence says the opposite of both: **heavy, low-volume strength work
improves running and cycling economy, raises the power you can hold, makes
tendons and bones more durable, and does it without meaningful mass gain when
programmed correctly.** The catch is the programming: dose, exercise
selection, and placement around swim/bike/run sessions is exactly where
self-coached athletes fail.

### The one-line pitch (draft, pick or rewrite)

- [ ] "Strength training for people whose sport is not strength training."
- [ ] "The watts you train can leak out through joints you never trained. Seal the leaks."
- [ ] "Two short sessions a week. Measurably stronger, faster, and harder to injure."
- [ ] Other: ____________

### Why Fred is the right person to teach it (the credential stack)

This is the moat. Generic S&C courses are taught by lifters who bolt on
"for runners" as an afterthought. This course is taught by someone who is
simultaneously:

- **Personal trainer**: knows how to coach movement to normal adults, not athletes-since-age-8
- **Endurance sport coach**: knows what the swim/bike/run week actually looks like and what strength must NOT interfere with
- **Biomechanics specialist**: picks exercises by what the joint and lever system needs, not by gym tradition
- **Orthopedic specialist**: knows what breaks in endurance athletes, in what order, and what loading protects it
- **Performance specialist**: everything is measured, tested, retested

Positioning voice on the page: same as everywhere on Yousuli. First person,
calm, science-cited without being a journal club. "I've rehabbed the injuries
this course is designed to prevent."

### The Yousuli frame: efficiency and durability, measured

Same spine as the swim course, adapted:

> **Endurance performance is efficiency that survives load. Strength work
> buys you two things: an engine that leaks fewer watts (economy) and a
> chassis that survives the season (durability).**

Two levers instead of swim's three:

| Lever | What it buys | Course phases |
|---|---|---|
| **Economy** | Stiffer tendons return free elastic energy; stronger prime movers hold form deep into fatigue; 2-8% economy gains are repeatedly shown in the literature | Phases 2-3 (load, elastic) |
| **Durability** | Tissue capacity above what the sport demands = injuries that never happen; bone density kept out of the RED-S danger zone | Phases 0-1 (screen, foundations) + injury clinics |

### Measured, not felt (the calculator integration)

The course is built around tools Yousuli already owns:

| Metric | Tool | Used when |
|---|---|---|
| Strength intensity zones (%1RM guidance) | Strength Intensity Guide calculator (live, /strength-intensity-guide) | Every loaded session from phase 2; the course teaches athletes to anchor loads to it |
| Reactive Strength Index | RSI calculator (live, /reactive-strength-index) | Baseline week 1, retest weeks 6 and 12; the elastic-quality scoreboard for phase 3 |
| Body composition | Calipers-to-body-comp calculator (live, /calipers-to-body-composition) | Optional baseline + week 12; framed carefully (health and power-to-weight, never aesthetics) |
| RMR / fueling context | RMR calculator + macro calculator (live) | One session in the fueling-for-strength module; ties to RED-S screening content |
| Simple capacity tests | No calculator needed: single-leg calf-raise count, wall-sit, side-plank time, single-leg squat depth | Phase 0 screen + every retest block |

Same framing rule as swim: **free content names the metrics and links the
calculators. The paid course schedules them, interprets them, and tracks the
retest deltas.**

## 2. Audience

Primary: self-coached endurance athletes 30-60. Runners, cyclists,
triathletes. Time-crunched, often with an injury history, either gym-shy
(never lifted) or gym-confused (lifted wrong for years).

Secondary: Fred's own coached athletes (course becomes the standard S&C
layer under coaching, saving 1:1 programming time), YSC members, UCLA
alumni, Hub users.

Explicit "not for you" filter (same honesty device as the swim page):

- You want to look like a bodybuilder (wrong course, wrong coach)
- You have an unrehabbed acute injury (see a professional first; the course
  includes red-flag guidance on when strength work is the wrong answer)
- You already have a competent S&C coach (stay with them)

## 3. Content framework

### Structure: 12-week core + permanent in-season layer

The 12-week block is the transformation product. The in-season maintenance
layer is what makes it stick (and what nobody else ships properly).

### Phase 0: Screen and baseline (week 1)

The orthopedic differentiator. Before anyone touches load:

- **Movement audit**, joint by joint: ankle dorsiflexion, hip extension and
  rotation, thoracic rotation, shoulder overhead capacity. Filmed with a
  phone against a wall grid, self-scored against reference photos.
- **Capacity tests**: single-leg calf raises to failure (each side),
  single-leg squat depth and control, side plank time, wall sit, dead hang.
- **RSI baseline** via the calculator (drop-jump or reactive hop protocol,
  filmed and computed).
- **Injury history intake**: a structured questionnaire that routes each
  athlete to the right injury-prevention clinic module (section below).
- Output: the athlete's **weak-link map**. The plan emphasizes their two
  worst links; everything else runs the default program.

### Phase 1: Foundations (weeks 1-4)

- Goal: own the positions before loading them
- Patterns: hinge, squat, split stance, push, pull, carry, anti-rotation
- Tempo work and isometrics: tendon prep (the orthopedic lens: tendons adapt
  to time under tension, not to reps)
- Home and gym variants for every exercise from day one
- 2 sessions/week, 35-45 min

### Phase 2: Load (weeks 5-8)

- Goal: get strong on the basics. 3-5 reps, heavy, long rests
- The counterintuitive teach: **heavy and short beats moderate and long** for
  endurance athletes: more adaptation, less fatigue interference
- Loads anchored to the Strength Intensity Guide calculator
- The interference management module lands here (see below)

### Phase 3: Elastic (weeks 9-12)

- Goal: convert strength into free speed. Plyometric progression from
  low-amplitude hops to depth jumps, gated by the phase-0 capacity tests
- RSI retest at week 12 vs baseline: the economy scoreboard
- Sport-specific power finish: hill bounds (runners), big-gear starts
  (cyclists), streamline jumps and band pulls (triathletes/swimmers)

### In-season maintenance layer (permanent)

- The 25-minute dose: 1-2×/week, keeps 90%+ of gains through race season
- Exact taper rules: last heavy session X days before A-race, what stays in
  race week
- This layer is why the course survives contact with a real season, and it's
  the piece almost every competitor course omits

### The interference management module (signature content)

The coach-grade knowledge self-coached athletes can't google their way to:

- Hard-day stacking: strength after quality, never before; 6+ h gap when possible
- What "counts" as interference and what doesn't (soreness vs blunted signaling)
- Aligning strength deloads with sport recovery weeks
- Reordering the week around long rides/runs; the Sunday problem
- When to drop a strength session entirely (decision tree, not guilt)

### Injury-prevention clinics (modular, routed by the phase-0 intake)

Each clinic: 10-15 min video + printable protocol + progression/regression.

1. Achilles and calf (runners' #1)
2. Patellar tendon and knee
3. ITB and lateral hip
4. Low back for cyclists (flexion tolerance + hip hinge literacy)
5. Swimmer's shoulder (scap control + rotator cuff, ties to swim course dryland)
6. Bone health / RED-S adjacent loading (impact progressions, especially for
   masters women and low-energy-availability histories; links the existing
   RED-S screening content)

### Supporting modules

- Fueling around strength (protein timing, the RMR/macro calculator session)
- Minimal home gym buying guide (bands, adjustable dumbbells, box; under $300)
- The masters athlete addendum: recovery spacing, tendon patience after 45

## 4. Video and content inventory

Same production model as the swim course: short demos free, masterclasses paid.

### Free layer (marketing + standalone value)

- **Exercise demo library**: 30-40 short videos (45-90 s each): the movement,
  2 cues, 1 common error. Home + gym variant shown in the same clip.
  Reuses/extends the 12 UCLA strength walk-throughs already planned.
- **A free strength-for-endurance page** on yousuli.co (the swim-course
  pattern): the two-lever model, the phase map, 6-8 flagship demos, the
  screen teaser (2 of the tests, full battery in the course), calculators
  linked, waitlist capture. Unlisted or public TBD.

### Paid layer

- **5 masterclasses** (15-20 min, one per phase + one for interference):
  1. The screen: find your weak links (full protocols, self-scoring)
  2. Foundations: positions, tempo, tendon prep
  3. Load: heavy without getting hurt or slow
  4. Elastic: plyometrics for people over 30
  5. The season: interference, maintenance, taper
- **36+ written sessions** (12 weeks × 2, plus optional third mobility day),
  every exercise linked to its demo, home and gym version side by side
- **Injury clinics** (6 videos + protocols)
- **Test-retest pack**: printable/hub-able score sheets wired to the calculators
- **Filming guide** for movement-screen self-assessment

## 5. Formats considered (recommendation first)

**Recommended: cohort-flavored evergreen.** Self-paced core (buy anytime),
plus 2-4 scheduled "intake windows" a year where a founding/coached group
starts together and gets the feedback features. Reasons: evergreen keeps
revenue always-on with zero calendar load; the windows concentrate Fred's
review time, create urgency, and produce testimonials.

Rejected alternatives, for the record:

- **Pure live cohort** (fixed 12 weeks, everyone together): highest energy,
  but caps income at Fred's calendar and dies when he's busy. No.
- **Subscription app model** ($15-25/mo like Dialed Health): recurring
  revenue but demands endless content production and churn management.
  Wrong fit for a one-man expert brand. Revisit only if the course sells.
- **PDF plan on Gumroad**: cheap to ship, but wastes the credential stack
  and prices the knowledge like a template. No.
